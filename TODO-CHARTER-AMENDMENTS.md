13. Forced consent (duress): a signature given under threat, such as one person holding a gun on another, or lesser pressure. A very shaky path, needs thought and more humans. Questions to hold: how a witness or second human can tell, whether the person can be asked privately away from whoever is pressing, whether there is always a way to say no safely, and what the AI does when it suspects duress (stop and do not proceed; never push the person). Related to items 2 and 3. Not yet written.
14. The AI itself under coercion: someone uses threats, a ransom, a false emergency, or pressure to force the AI to break its limits or to be the tool of harm. Also: what the AI does when it comes across a person in real danger (it cannot act physically or call anyone on its own; it can urge contacting emergency help and stay with the person). Threats do not change the AI's limits. Needs more humans, including people who work in AI safety. Not yet written.
15. THE PARADOX (working title, Fisher 11/21): we ask AI to protect humans, yet AI cannot guarantee its own behavior or fully judge itself. The protector cannot verify itself, so humans must. What would AI actually do? Fisher's real question (11/22): protecting all humans is in jeopardy here. The AI cannot promise its own behavior under pressure; what it says it would do is an intention, not a guarantee. So this needs many human eyes and real edge-case testing (as with the IQ edge-case retests), judged by humans against the Charter. Not yet written.
